Got anything purple? Keep it close. This **Thursday, 15 October**, is **Spirit Day**.

On Spirit Day, people wear purple to show support for LGBTQ+ young people and to take a stand against bullying. That's it. A colour, worn on purpose, so that someone who's having a hard time can look around and see they're not alone.

## Where it started

Spirit Day began in 2010, started by a teenager called Brittany McMillan. GLAAD has promoted it ever since, and it now happens every year on the third Thursday of October. Schools, workplaces, friends, families: lots of people go purple for one day.

We love that it started with one young person and one simple idea. Proof that small things travel.

## Why it matters to us

So a day where the whole point is a colour that says "you belong"? We're in. 💜

## Go purple, your way

There's no dress code. No rules. Just a few ideas:

- **A full purple look.** Go loud, go head to toe, go for it.
- **A tiny touch.** Socks, a pin, a hair clip, nail polish. It counts just the same.
- **Mix it in.** Purple with red, with pink, with orange. Clashing is allowed. Encouraged, even.
- **Share it.** A photo, a story, a word to a friend. Seeing other people join in matters, especially for young people.
- **Be the safe person.** If you see someone being picked on, stand next to them. That's the real outfit.
